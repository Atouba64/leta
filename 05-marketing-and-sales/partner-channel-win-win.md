# Partner channel & win-win model — Leta

This document captures Leta’s **go-to-market thesis** with **fulfillment partners** (legacy dispatch and field-service companies that already own customer relationships and ticket volume) and the **three-sided** value loop: partners, technicians, and end customers.

## The core insight

Large service organizations (examples include **Barrister Group LLC** and similar national or regional dispatch networks) typically have:

- **Strong demand:** large client databases, recurring contracts, and steady work-order volume.  
- **A chronic constraint:** **not enough qualified on-site technicians** in every geography—especially when SLAs require coverage across **Georgia** metros, suburbs, and secondary cities.

Leta is built to sit **alongside** those companies as a **capacity and transparency layer**: we help them **fulfill** work orders they already sold, in a **reasonable mile radius**, with a **modern app** so they can see status, proof of work, and outcomes—without replacing their brand or customer contract in the partner-led model.

**Positioning:** Leta is **not** framed as zero-sum competition with these partners. It is **coopetition that strengthens both sides**: they bring **demand** (and trust with enterprise buyers); Leta brings **supply orchestration** (Georgia field network + product) and **operational visibility**.

## What Leta brings to fulfillment partners

| Partner need | How Leta helps |
|--------------|----------------|
| **Tech shortage** | Access to a **Georgia-wide** pool of vetted field professionals, coordinated through one platform. |
| **SLA risk** | Geo-aware matching so tickets are more likely to be **accepted and completed** inside the window. |
| **Opaque field execution** | **Transparency:** dashboard-style visibility (tracking, artifacts, signatures—per contract and privacy rules). |
| **Cost pressure** | Better utilization and less “last mile” chaos can **reduce** rework, truck rolls, and partner-side coordination overhead. |
| **Customer satisfaction** | Faster, more predictable onsite delivery **improves** the partner’s relationship with **their** client. |

The founder’s **years of experience in the field** and relationships across Georgia are a practical moat: Leta is not starting cold on supply.

## Demand aggregation: why techs see “abundance of work”

Leta combines **two demand streams** into one marketplace liquidity pool:

1. **Partner-originated work orders** — tickets partners cannot staff internally fast enough.  
2. **Direct customer orders** — individuals and businesses that open the app and request **IT service on demand** (DoorDash / Uber mental model: **order a tech**, track the visit, pay digitally).

Technicians benefit because the same **Active** pool can receive offers from **both** streams. The product goal is **steady, defensible work** so professionals can treat Leta as a **primary income channel**, not a sporadic side gig—subject to market depth and onboarding quality.

## Win-win-win (stakeholder outcomes)

### For field technicians

- **More work orders** from aggregated partner volume **plus** direct consumer/B2C demand.  
- **Less idle time** when the network effects kick in (Georgia density strategy).  
- **Modern tooling:** clear offers, payouts, escalation to remote experts—reducing administrative friction.

### For partners (e.g. Barrister-class and regional equivalents)

- **Higher fulfillment rate** within a defined radius of each job.  
- **Assured delivery** as a *process goal* (SLA-backed operations, not hand-wavy promises).  
- **Speed and clarity** for their operations teams; happier **their** end customers.  
- **Growth without linear hiring** of W-2 bench in every zip code.

### For end customers (individuals & companies ordering service)

- **Qualified tech, faster** — driven by liquidity + clear matching rules.  
- **More predictable experience** — transparency, communication, and structured close-out.  
- **Competitive economics** — marketplace efficiency and reduced rework can translate to **better effective price** than fragmented phone-tag models (exact pricing is policy and remains to be validated in market).

## Messaging guardrails (external)

- Emphasize **partnership** and **fulfillment** language with enterprise partners; avoid “disruption” tropes that sound like channel conflict.  
- For customers, lead with **on-demand IT** clarity: “**Leta is IT service on demand**—Georgia first” (consumer analogy: DoorDash for IT, with professional scope and SLAs where applicable).  
- Always tie claims to **measurable** pilot metrics as they become available (ETA, fill rate, CSAT, first-time fix).
