# Phone script — Enver (Barrister) · first partnership call

**Use this file on the call.** Read it once before dialing; then speak naturally — do not read word-for-word like a robot.

**Contact:** Enver · **Associate** (confirm exact title and department on call)  
**Company:** Barrister Global Services Network · [barrister.com](https://barrister.com/)  
**Your goal:** Earn a **next step** (intro to vendor/dispatch lead, 15-min demo, or agreement to scope a **Georgia pilot**) — not a signed contract on this call.

---

## Before you dial (2 minutes)

- [ ] Quiet space; phone charged; notepad or this doc open  
- [ ] Smile — it carries in your voice  
- [ ] Know your **one-sentence ask:** “5–10 ticket pilot in Georgia with your work order numbers — can you point me to who scopes that?”  
- [ ] Have open: [leta.repair/partners](https://leta.repair/partners.html) (if he asks “website”)  
- [ ] **Do not** trash Barrister, tech pay, or individuals — **coopetition** only  

---

## If voicemail (30 seconds)

> “Hi Enver, this is **[Your name]** with **Leta** — L-E-T-A. We help national field networks with **Georgia onsite IT fulfillment** and **live job visibility** for dispatch. I’m not selling a rip-and-replace — we’re looking at a **small pilot** as overflow where bench or status calls are painful. My number is **(470) 252-6681**. I’ll follow up by email to **partners@leta.repair**. Thanks.”

---

## When Enver answers — opening (30–45 seconds)

**Tone:** Calm, respectful, peer-to-peer — you’re an operator, not a telemarketer.

> “Hi Enver, this is **[Your name]** with **Leta** — is this a good time for a quick call, maybe **three or four minutes**? I’ll be brief.”

**If “Who is Leta?” / “What’s this about?”**

> “We’re a **Georgia-first platform** for onsite IT fulfillment. Partners keep the **customer and the SLA**; we provide **local bench techs**, **live status** on each work order, and **ticket-level communication** so dispatch isn’t playing phone tag all day. I’m reaching out because firms at **Barrister’s scale** often have **plenty of demand** but still feel pain in **specific markets** — especially **visibility and staffing certain radii**. I’d love to learn if that’s true for you in the Southeast, and whether a **low-risk pilot** would even be worth discussing.”

**If “How did you get my number?”**

> “[If referral:] **[Name]** suggested I speak with you about partnership / vendor routing.  
> [If cold:] I’m mapping **field partnership** contacts at networks that handle enterprise break-fix — your name came up as someone who works with **associates / vendors / partners**. If I’m off base on your role, I’d appreciate being pointed to the right desk.”

**If “I’m busy”**

> “Totally understand. Can I grab **90 seconds** now, or is there a better **15-minute window** this week? I’m not asking for a contract — just the right person for a **Georgia overflow pilot**.”

---

## Build credibility (45–60 seconds)

> “I’ll be direct. I’ve spent time in the **Barrister-style** world — national dispatch, **GUS-style** tickets where the **partner work order number** has to survive every hop, **POC-only** sites, Teams bridges when the job gets hard. Leta was built to fix the **gray area**: the customer doesn’t know when the tech is coming, dispatch gets status calls, and the field tech is stuck between tiers.”

> “We’re **not** trying to be another national broker with fifteen thousand names in a database. We’re building **density and modern tooling in Georgia** — **skills-based** tech profiles, **distance-aware** dispatch so you’re not sending someone a hundred miles blind, **partner portal** visibility, and **logged** partner-to-tech messages so your team isn’t the switchboard.”

**Optional (only if he’s engaged):**

> “Think **overflow and specialist routing** when your network is thin — and **fewer ‘where’s the tech?’ calls** when jobs are live. That’s the partnership conversation.”

---

## Why Barrister should care (pick 2–3 — don’t lecture)

| Say this | Why it lands |
|----------|----------------|
| “**Georgia bench** when your roster is thin in certain counties.” | Capacity without insulting national scale |
| “**Status in the portal** — en route, on site, complete — before anyone calls your 1-800.” | Dispatch pain you can prove in pilot |
| “**Economy break-fix** or **specialist** — you tag the WO; we match skill level.” | Margin control |
| “**Your ticket number** on every job — audit for upstream retail or IT.” | GUS respect |
| “**Five to ten tickets** to prove it — not a national RFP.” | Low risk |

**Script block:**

> “What we’re proposing isn’t ‘replace Barrister.’ It’s: when you have **Georgia work** — or **tight SLA** jobs where **visibility** matters — route a **pilot batch** through Leta. You keep **your work order ID**. We show **timeline, photos, sign-off**. You measure whether **status calls** drop and **on-time** improves. If the data’s ugly, we part friends. If it’s good, we talk **rate card** and **more counties**.”

---

## What you want from Enver (clear ask)

> “Enver, I’d like your help with two things:  
> **One** — is **Georgia / Southeast overflow** even a problem Barrister cares about right now, or am I barking up the wrong tree?  
> **Two** — if it is, who is the right person for a **short pilot conversation** — **vendor management**, **dispatch operations**, or someone on your team?  
> I’m happy to send a **one-page pilot outline** and do a **15-minute screen share** of the partner view.”

**Pause. Listen.**

---

## Discovery (if he’s talking — use his words back)

Ask 2–3 from [`discovery-questions.md`](./discovery-questions.md):

- “How does dispatch today know a tech is **on the way**?”  
- “Which **areas in Georgia** are hardest to fill?”  
- “When you add a **subcontractor or overflow partner**, what’s the **approval path**?”

**Reflect:** “So it sounds like **[repeat his pain]** — that’s exactly what we built the portal and ticket thread for.”

---

## Handle pushback (short)

| He says | You say |
|---------|---------|
| “We have enough techs.” | “Nationwide, I believe you. I’m asking about **moments when GA is thin** or **dispatch is on the phone** — is that ever true?” |
| “Email me.” | “Absolutely — what’s the best address? And can we put **15 minutes** on the calendar so I’m not just a PDF in the inbox?” |
| “Not my department.” | “Who owns **vendor or field partnerships**? Can you **warm intro** or should I mention you referred me?” |
| “We don’t use subs.” | “Understood — is there a **preferred partner program** for capacity, or should I speak with **operations** instead?” |

Full list: [`objections-and-responses.md`](./objections-and-responses.md)

## Close the call (always do this)

> “Here’s what I’ll do: I’ll send a **short email** today — pilot outline, link to **leta.repair/partners**, and my direct line **(470) 252-6681**.  
> What I’d like from you: **either** a name for the right stakeholder **or** a **yes** to a **15-minute follow-up** — **[suggest two times, e.g. Thursday 10am or Friday 2pm Eastern]**.  
> Does either work, or what’s better for you?”

**If yes to meeting:**

> “Great — I’ll send a calendar invite to **[email]**.”

**If name only:**

> “Thank you — I’ll mention you connected us. Anything I should **not** say when I call them? Any **priority** for them — SLA, cost, or visibility?”

**If hard no:**

> “I appreciate your time. When do you typically **revisit vendors or regional capacity**? I’ll check back **[quarter / six months]** unless you’d prefer I don’t.”

## Cheat sheet — phrases that win partnerships

**Use:**

- “**Overflow** and **visibility**”  
- “**You keep the customer story**”  
- “**Pilot** — five to ten tickets”  
- “**Your work order number** on every ticket”  
- “**Locked rate at accept**”  
- “**Fewer status calls**”  
- “**Georgia first** — prove it, then expand”  

**Avoid:**

- “Barrister is broken” / “your techs are exploited”  
- “We’ll put you out of business”  
- “National rollout next month”  
- “Free work” without defining pilot scope  

---

## Your personal close (optional — if authentic)

> “Enver, we’re a **small Georgia team** building the product dispatch should have had ten years ago. I’d rather **earn** a pilot with data than talk in circles. If you give us a shot on **a handful of tickets**, we’ll make your dispatch look good — that’s the whole point.”
