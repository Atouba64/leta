# Competitor forensics — Field Nation & WorkMarket (ADP)

The broader field service industry is heavily reliant on freelance management systems and gig-economy platforms. The two dominant players in this sector are **Field Nation** and **WorkMarket by ADP**. While these platforms theoretically connect supply (technicians) with demand (buyers), they have devolved into highly extractive middlemen that damage both the worker's profitability and the end-customer's quality of service.

## Field Nation

Field Nation presents itself as an open marketplace, but its fundamental business model is built on margin extraction. The platform charges the field technician a massive **10% fee** on every single work order simply to process the payment.

Because the platform is open and unvetted, it has become oversaturated with unskilled labor. This oversaturation creates a "race to the bottom" where unqualified individuals consistently underbid highly certified professionals. Consequently, high-quality enterprise buyers are forced to sift through hundreds of underqualified applicants, delaying critical deployments and increasing the risk of failed installations.

## WorkMarket by ADP

WorkMarket by ADP is marketed as an enterprise-grade "freelance management system" that helps businesses organize "Labor Clouds," automate onboarding, verify credentials, and manage tax compliance like 1099-NEC filings.

While it offers robust backend administrative tools, the platform is frequently weaponized by middleman companies. Firms utilize WorkMarket to insert themselves between the enterprise client and the technician, shaving down the technician's hourly rate to unsustainable levels (e.g., $45/hour) to pad their own corporate profits.

Because WorkMarket serves primarily as administrative software, it lacks any inherent intelligence to stop punitive dispatching or guarantee equitable compensation, making it an enabler of bad industry behavior rather than a solution.
